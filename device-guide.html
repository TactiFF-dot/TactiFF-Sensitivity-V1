Device Sensitivity Guide

Your device can affect how gaming sensitivity feels. Screen size, refresh rate, touch response, performance and control layout can all influence aiming and camera movement.

Why Device Matters

The same sensitivity value may feel different on two different devices. This is why sensitivity should be treated as a starting point rather than a universal setting.

Screen Size

Screen size can affect how far your finger travels during aiming and camera movement. Players using different screen sizes may prefer different sensitivity levels.

Refresh Rate

A higher refresh rate can make movement appear smoother when the device and game support it. However, refresh rate alone does not determine the best sensitivity.

Touch Response

Touch response and input processing can influence how quickly your movements appear on screen. Consistent touch input can make sensitivity adjustments easier to evaluate.

Device Performance

Frame-rate stability and overall device performance can affect the consistency of gameplay. If performance is unstable, sensitivity changes may not feel consistent from one session to another.

Tip: Test sensitivity after your device has reached a stable performance state instead of changing several settings at the same time.

Low-End Devices

On lower-performance devices, focus on stable gameplay and consistent controls. Avoid making large sensitivity changes while also changing multiple performance settings.

High Refresh-Rate Devices

Higher refresh-rate devices can provide smoother visual feedback when supported by the game and hardware. Your preferred sensitivity should still be based on control and consistency rather than refresh rate alone.

How to Find a Comfortable Setting

Start with a balanced sensitivity profile, perform a drag calibration test, and then use aim testing to evaluate your control. Make small adjustments instead of changing everything at once.
